Sources

Three crates. One dig.

No more tab-hopping across sample sites. Sampler queries every royalty-free source at once and ranks what comes back.

Pixabay Sound Effects SOURCE

Stems, one-shots, and field recordings from Pixabay's royalty-free library. Search it like you think: "thunder lightning", "metal riffs".

Freesound SOURCE

The deep community archive. Obscure percussion, foley, and textures you will not find in a commercial pack.

Internet Archive SOURCE

The public-domain vault. Historical recordings and raw audio, free to sample and flip.

Analysis

Analyzed, not just listed.

Every result runs through on-device analysis, so you know the tempo and the chops before you download anything.

BPM + Key ON DEVICE

Client-side tempo and key detection on each hit, pinned to 60 to 180 BPM. Sort your results by what fits the track, not by upload date.

Slice points ONSET

Transient detection finds the hits inside a sample, so a loop arrives pre-chopped and ready to rearrange.

Find similar RANKING

Pick a result you like and re-rank the whole crate by musical similarity. One good snare finds you twenty more.

Slice grids MIDI

Slices map chromatically to MIDI with gapless tiling and crossfades. Play a chopped loop from your keyboard like an instrument.

Export

Straight into the session.

Analysis travels with the sample. Export a track's BPM, key, slices, and loops, then drop the file into your DAW workflow.

# one command, two artifacts
sampler export <track-id> --format json --out kick.sampler.json
sampler export <track-id> --format csv  --out kick.markers.csv

JSON sidecar carries the full analysis record: BPM, key, slices, loops, markers. Markers CSV is bar, beat, time, label, kind, ready for timeline import. Both come from the same zero-dependency engine that powers the browser UI.

FAQ

Asked, answered.

Where do the samples come from? +
Three royalty-free sources: Pixabay Sound Effects, Freesound, and the Internet Archive audio collection. One search queries all of them.
Can I use the samples in released music? +
Every result carries its own license from its source. Check the license on the samples you use before shipping a record.
Do I need API keys? +
No. Bring your own keys for the sources you use, or connect through DOGS.
What does "analyzed" mean? +
On-device BPM and key detection for each result, plus transient slice-point detection, so you see tempo and slice markers before you download anything.
What is the export for? +
sampler export turns a track's analysis into a JSON sidecar or a markers CSV you can drop into a DAW session.
